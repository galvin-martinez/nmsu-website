================================================================
 ROASTING AND FREEZING HATCH GREEN CHILE
 Straight from the garden, for keeping all year
================================================================

WHEN
  Late August through September. That is the whole season, so
  when you see them, buy more than you think you need. Everyone
  who has ever said "a half bushel is plenty" was wrong.

HEAT LEVELS
  Mild      good for people who are still learning
  Medium    the honest default
  Hot       what you actually want
  X-Hot     what you claim you want in front of company

WHAT YOU NEED
  Fresh Hatch green chile
  A hot fire: propane roaster, gas grill, or an oven broiler
  Tongs
  A large bowl and a lid, or paper grocery bags
  Freezer bags
  Gloves. Yes, gloves. Ask anyone who has skipped this step
  and then rubbed an eye four hours later.

ROASTING
  1. Get the heat high. Chile wants to blister fast, not cook
     slowly. Slow heat gives you mush.
  2. Lay the pods out in one layer. Do not crowd them.
  3. Roast until the skin blisters and blackens in patches and
     the pod starts to collapse. Roughly 4 to 6 minutes a side
     on a grill, less on a real roaster.
  4. Turn them once. Both sides should have color. You are not
     trying to burn the flesh, only the skin.

STEAMING (do not skip)
  5. Move the hot pods straight into a bowl and cover it, or
     into a paper bag and roll it shut.
  6. Let them sit 10 to 15 minutes. The trapped steam lifts the
     skin off the flesh. This is the step that decides whether
     peeling is pleasant or miserable.

FREEZING
  7. Here is the part most people get wrong: freeze them with
     the skins ON. The skin protects the flesh from freezer
     burn, and it slips off easily after thawing.
  8. Portion into freezer bags in amounts you would actually
     use at once. A year of chile in one giant brick helps
     nobody.
  9. Press the air out. Label with the heat level and the year,
     because by February you will not remember either.
 10. Good for about a year. It will not last a year.

USING IT
  Thaw, slip the skins off, pull the stems, and chop. Seeds
  stay if you want the heat, go if you do not.

  Goes in: eggs, burgers, stew, mac and cheese, cornbread,
  anything with a tortilla near it, and at least one thing your
  friends from out of state will describe as "a lot."
